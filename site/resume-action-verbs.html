Why verbs matter more than adjectives

“Results-driven professional” tells a recruiter nothing. “Reduced onboarding time from 14 days to 6 by rebuilding the checklist and automating approvals” tells them what you did, how, and what changed. The verb is the spine of that sentence. Choose it before you add the detail.

Use past tense for previous roles and present tense for your current role. Put the most important verb first in the bullet. If you cannot find a verb that is both accurate and specific, the bullet may be too vague to keep.

Frequently asked questions

What are the strongest resume action verbs?

The strongest verb is the one that accurately describes what you did and is followed by a measurable result. Led, built, shipped, reduced, increased, automated, negotiated, and diagnosed are strong because they imply ownership and an outcome.

Should every bullet start with a verb?

Almost every experience bullet should start with a past-tense verb for previous roles and a present-tense verb for your current role. Avoid starting with “Responsible for”, “Helped with”, or “Worked on”.

Can I use the same verb twice?

Repeating a verb is fine when it is the accurate word. Variety for its own sake produces awkward bullets. Use different verbs when they describe genuinely different actions.
