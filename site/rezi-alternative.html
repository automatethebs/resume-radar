A lighter Rezi alternative for one ATS check.

Rezi is an AI resume builder with templates, rewriting tools, and a free tier. ResumeRadar does one narrower job: it scores the resume you already have against a specific posting, shows the keyword gaps, and explains the fixes — without an account, an upload, or a subscription.

The short version

If you want AI writing help and a template library, Rezi is the more complete product. It offers a free plan for one resume, a $29/month Pro plan for unlimited work, and a $149 lifetime option. If you want to test the resume you already wrote against the job you are about to apply for, ResumeRadar is smaller on purpose: no account, no document storage, and a one-time price.

When Rezi is the better choice

Choose Rezi if the resume itself needs work and you want help rewriting it. Its free plan includes the AI resume writer and editor, one resume, one AI interview, and three PDF downloads, which is enough to test the workflow. Pro at $29/month removes the resume and download limits and unlocks the full Rezi Score and keyword targeting. The $149 lifetime plan is worth comparing with a few months of Pro if you expect to keep using the builder.

When ResumeRadar is the better choice

Choose ResumeRadar if you do not want another account or another AI rewriting your experience. Paste the resume text and the posting, and the scanner shows what a parser can read, which important terms are missing, and which fixes are worth making first. The scan is free, Pro is $9 once, and nothing is uploaded. That makes it a low-commitment second opinion before you apply.

What the free scan actually shows

The free report gives you a 0–100 score, four category scores, the top five missing keywords, parser warnings, and the first four fixes. Pro adds every missing keyword ranked by importance, the complete role checklist, every fix, a cover-letter outline, eight interview questions, and a downloadable text report. There is no subscription and no recurring charge.

Frequently asked questions

Is ResumeRadar a Rezi alternative?

For one task, yes: checking a resume against a specific job posting. ResumeRadar gives you a score, missing keywords, parser warnings, and fixes without an account or resume upload. Rezi is the better fit if you want an AI resume builder, designed templates, and a place to store and rewrite resumes inside an account.

How much does Rezi cost?

Rezi advertises a free plan at $0, a Pro plan at $29 per month, and a Lifetime plan at $149 one-time when we checked on November 14, 2026. Rezi sets its own prices and they can change, so verify the current offer on Rezi's pricing page before buying.

What does Rezi include for free?

At the time of checking, Rezi's free plan included 1 resume, 1 AI interview, 3 PDF downloads, standard format templates, and the AI resume writer and editor. The Rezi Score and keyword targeting were listed as limited on the free plan and full on Pro.

Does ResumeRadar require an account?

No. You paste resume text and a job description, and the scan runs in your browser. There is no signup, no resume upload endpoint, and no server-side resume storage. Pro is a one-time $9 unlock, not a subscription.

Which one should I use?

Use Rezi if you want AI help rewriting the resume itself and a template library, and you are comfortable creating an account. Use ResumeRadar if you already have resume text you trust and want one private, low-cost check of the posting in front of you.
