The formula

Who you are. What changed. What is next.

  • Who: role, years, and three relevant skills.
  • Proof: one result with a number or clear scope.
  • Direction: the problem you want to work on next.

Keep it to 40–70 words. Write it after your experience bullets, so the proof is already on the page. Never claim a skill you cannot defend in an interview.

Why a summary is not an objective statement

“Seeking a challenging role where I can grow” describes what you want from an employer. A summary should describe what an employer gets. Lead with the role and the evidence, not with a wish. The generator uses the same role vocabulary as the 39 keyword guides because that is the language both a parser and a recruiter are scanning for.

Frequently asked questions

Is the summary generator free?

Yes. It runs in your browser with no account and no email gate. The optional Pro report is a separate $9 upgrade; the generator and the summaries it produces are free.

Does the generator use AI?

No. It uses deterministic templates built from the role vocabulary in the 39 role guides. That means the output is predictable and private, but you still need to add your own true evidence. It will not invent experience for you.

How do I make the summary sound less generic?

Replace the bracketed result with a real number and scope, then cut any skill you would not want to be interviewed on. The strongest summary is one specific sentence about a result, not a list of adjectives.

Should I use one summary for every application?

Change at least the first sentence. Mirror the job title and the top two or three requirements from the posting. The rest of the summary can stay stable if it is accurate.
