Why this matters

Recruiters search for phrases, not vibes.

An applicant tracking system turns your resume into searchable fields. If the posting says “observability” and your resume says “monitoring”, the filter may never surface you. The extractor shows the vocabulary the posting actually uses so you can mirror it honestly.

What the extractor does

It tokenizes the posting, removes generic filler, boosts known skills and tools, and groups repeated two- and three-word phrases into a ranked list. The result is not a magic ATS score; it is a vocabulary map. Use it to decide which terms deserve a place in your summary, skills section, and most recent role.

How to use keywords without stuffing

  • Start with the top five. If you can truthfully show them in your most recent role, do that first.
  • Write evidence, not a list. “Built a React dashboard used by 12,000 monthly customers” beats “React, dashboards, customers”.
  • Match the exact phrase where it is honest. Synonyms help humans; exact terms help filters.
  • Leave out what you cannot defend. An interview is a terrible place to discover that a keyword was aspirational.

Frequently asked questions

How does the keyword extractor work?

It splits the posting into words and short phrases, removes generic language, boosts known role skills, and ranks the remaining terms by frequency and relevance. Everything runs locally in your browser.

Should I put every keyword on my resume?

No. Use only the terms that truthfully describe your experience. A keyword list is a vocabulary guide, not a checklist to paste. Recruiters and interviewers will ask about anything you claim.

Is the keyword extractor free?

Yes. It is free with no account and no upload. The optional Pro report adds a full resume gap analysis, the complete role checklist, and a downloadable report.

Does it work for non-tech jobs?

Yes. Choose a role from the list or leave it on general auto-detect. The extractor uses the posting itself, so it works for nursing, teaching, sales, accounting, operations, design, and engineering roles.
