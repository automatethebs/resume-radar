Format guide

The ATS resume format that actually parses.

Most formatting advice is cosmetic. These are the rules that change whether a parser can read your resume at all — and the ones that quietly cost you interviews.

What an ATS actually reads

An applicant tracking system receives your file, extracts the text, guesses which piece is your name, contact details, employers, titles, dates, education, and skills, then stores the result in a searchable database. Recruiters filter that database by keyword, title, location, and years of experience.

That is the whole trick. Most rejections are not a robot shouting “no” — they are a recruiter searching for “Kubernetes” and never seeing a resume that says “container orchestration”. The format determines whether your evidence reaches the search index in the first place.

The nine rules

  1. Use a single column. Many parsers read left to right. A two-column layout can merge unrelated lines or drop your skills into the middle of your experience.
  2. Use standard section headings. “Experience”, “Education”, “Skills”, and “Projects” are anchors. Creative labels like “My journey” are a gamble.
  3. Keep contact details in the body. Your name, email, phone, city, and links belong on the first page, not in the document header or footer. Some parsers skip those regions entirely.
  4. Use month and year dates. “Mar 2021 – Present” is parseable. “2021” alone is ambiguous and can make your timeline look shorter than it is.
  5. Use a common font at 10–12pt. Arial, Calibri, Helvetica, Georgia, and Times New Roman are safe. Decorative fonts can turn into garbled characters.
  6. Avoid tables, text boxes, and images. Text inside them may be skipped, duplicated, or read out of order. Icons and skill rating bars carry no machine-readable meaning.
  7. Put skills in plain text. A comma-separated list is ideal. No logos, no stars, no “expert / advanced” sliders.
  8. Export a selectable PDF or DOCX. Open the file, select the text, and paste it into a plain-text editor. If the words disappear or arrive as symbols, the parser is seeing the same mess.
  9. Name the file clearly. Firstname-Lastname-Resume.pdf is easier for a human and a system than final_final_v7.pdf.

PDF or DOCX?

Either can work if the text is selectable. A text-based PDF preserves the layout and is usually the safer default. DOCX is useful when a portal explicitly asks for it or when a recruiter may need to edit. The format matters less than the content layer: a scanned PDF, an image export, or a PDF with outlined fonts gives the parser nothing.

The skeleton is intentionally plain. That is the point: the structure should be invisible to the reader and obvious to the parser.

What to remove

  • Photos and logos. They add file size and parsing risk, and US employers generally do not want them.
  • Skill rating bars. “JavaScript: 4/5 stars” means nothing to a recruiter and less to a parser.
  • Tables and text boxes. They are the most common cause of scrambled resume text.
  • Headers and footers for contact info. Put the important details in the body.
  • Walls of text. Short bullets with a strong verb and a measurable result are easier for humans and machines.

Test the file before you apply

Open your exported resume, select all, and paste it into a plain-text editor. If the order is scrambled, the dates disappear, or the skills merge into the experience section, fix the file before sending it anywhere. Frequently asked questions

Should I use PDF or DOCX for an ATS?

Either can work if the text is selectable. A text-based PDF preserves layout and is usually the safer default. DOCX is useful when a portal explicitly asks for it or when a recruiter may need to edit. Never submit a scanned image or a PDF with outlined text.

Do columns break an ATS?

They can. Many parsers read left to right, so a two-column layout can merge unrelated lines or put your skills in the middle of your experience. A single-column layout is the safest choice for a machine-read resume.

Can I use a resume template from Canva?

Yes, if it exports selectable text and avoids tables, text boxes, icons, and multi-column layouts. Many designed templates fail those checks. Test the exported file by selecting the text and pasting it into a plain-text editor.

Where should my contact details go?

Put your name, email, phone, city, and relevant links in the body of the first page, not in the document header. Some parsers skip headers and footers entirely.

Quick checklist

  • Single column
  • Standard headings
  • Contact details in the body
  • Month + year dates
  • Selectable text
  • No tables or text boxes
  • Plain-text skills
  • PDF or DOCX
