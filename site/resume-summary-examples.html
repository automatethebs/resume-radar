A summary is not an objective statement. It is the first evidence a recruiter reads. Steal the structure, replace the brackets with true specifics, and keep the measurable result.

The three-sentence formula

  1. Who you are. Title, years of experience, and the three skills the posting cares about most.
  2. Proof. One measurable result with a verb, a scope, and an outcome. A number is better than an adjective.
  3. Direction. The kind of role or problem you want next — specific enough to sound intentional.

Keep it to 40–70 words. Write it last, after the experience bullets, so the proof is already on the page. Never claim a skill you would not want to be interviewed on.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a resume summary be?

Two to four sentences. One sentence for your role and years of experience, one or two for proof, and one for what you want next. A summary that runs past five lines is usually hiding a cover letter.

Should I include a summary if I have little experience?

Yes, if you have something concrete to say. For early-career candidates, lead with education, projects, or a measurable result instead of claiming years you do not have. Skip the summary entirely if it would only repeat your headline.

Do I need a different summary for every job?

Not every word, but the first sentence should change. Mirror the title and the top two or three requirements from the posting. Reusing one summary across every application is one of the cheapest mistakes to fix.
